# The World — a lore bible

Everything in this game is original coinage. This file is the canon: what things
are called, who they are, and how the names hang together. When adding content,
extend this world — never borrow from someone else's.

## The setting: four acts and the Abyss

The world is crossed east to west by the caravan road. Each act is a land with a
town, a wild surface, and a descent of six floors; the act's ruler holds the last.

| Act | Land | Town | The descent |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Mournfield** | **Hollowmere** | the cathedral floors |
| II | **The Sunscar Wastes** | **Zerath Oasis** | **the Sunken Necropolis** (tombs) |
| III | **The Drowned Green** | **Mirefall Landing** | **the Ziggurat of Ssarim** |
| IV | **The Hollow Throne** | **The Last Vigil** | **the Hollowdeep** — and victory |
| V | **The Endless Abyss** | — | below everything, forever |

The same company travels with you — Torvald's forge, Lysa's stall, Fenwick's
crooked table, Maren's Wardens — packed onto the caravan at every border. The
**waypoint network** binds it all: stones stand in every town and on floors 3
and 5 of every descent; touch one and it answers you anywhere, forever.

- **Hollowmere** — the first town. A walled settlement at the edge of the wood;
  the last warm light before the first descent.
- **The Mournfield** — the forest outside Hollowmere's gate. Also drifts
  into **The Blackbough** and **The Forgotten Glade**.
- **Mount Kragmar** — the sacred peak of the old clans; the mountain the
  barbarians swear by (*Wrath of the Mountain*, *Kragmar's Face*, *Kragfall*).
- **The Ternion Order** — a vanished order of alchemist-scholars who worked
  transmutation in threes. Their **Ternion Altar** stands in town: three identical
  runes become the next tier; three magic items become an unidentified rare.
- **The Hidden Meadow** — there is no meadow. (A certain peg leg, taken to the
  Ternion Altar, opens the way to it. Moo.)

## The Hollow Kings

Three brother-kings who traded their names to something in the dark and now rule
the deep floors. Their servants and generals guard every descent.

| Boss | Title | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hargol the Flenser** | butcher of the cathedral steps | "Meat for the hooks!" |
| **Corvessa, the Carrion Bride** | pale archeress | kites, cold arrows |
| **The Maggot Queen** | mother of the brood | spawns adds |
| **Vessira, the Thorned Maiden** | venom nova | "Every wound you carry, I planted." |
| **Vuruk, the White Maw** | charging chiller | his hits carry the cold |
| **Orvath, the Grudge Eternal** | lightning orbs, keeps distance | "Spite outlives the spine." |
| **Malgrim, the King of Dread** | first of the Hollow Kings | dread-fire nova |
| **Aldric the Forsworn** | the knight who chose the dark | "I remember the light. I chose this." |
| **Uldrath, the Unmaker** | last of the Hollow Kings | "I am what remains when all else is finished." |
| **Bovax, Tyrant of the Meadow** | there is no meadow | "MOO." |
| **Queen Nyzat, the Embalmed** | Act II's ruler, below the Necropolis | "I have worn this sand for a thousand years." |
| **Ssarim, the Drowned God** | Act III's ruler, heart of the Ziggurat | "The river only ever asks once." |

Act placements: Hargol prowls the Mournfield; Vessira ends Act I. Vuruk waits in
the middle tombs and Nyzat ends Act II. Orvath broods mid-temple and Ssarim ends
Act III. Aldric, then Malgrim, then **Uldrath** — unmade at the bottom of Act IV,
which opens the Endless Abyss.

- **The Hollow Pilgrim** — a roaming super-elite that rarely walks a floor; may
  drop **The Pilgrim's Burden**, the unique charm.

## The bestiary (original species)

**imp** (cowardly pack scavengers — scatter when a packmate dies), **spineback**
(spits spines, keeps its distance), **goatman**, **hellion** (the big fire
brutes of the deep). The wastes: **scarab**, **sandserpent**, **the entombed**,
**duneprowler**, **carrionbird**. The green: **mudling**, **blowreed hunter**,
**riverfang**, **vinehorror**, **bogwitch**. The Throne: **ashfiend**,
**doomguard**, **flamecaller**, **the flayed**. Generic mythology (zombie,
skeleton, wraith, gargoyle, succubus, demon, cultist, goblin, bovine) stays
generic.

## The rune alphabet

Twenty runes, depth-ascending — the last seven surrendered only by the
sharpened worlds:
**Vek → Ond → Rok → Fyr → Zel → Hym → Mur → Vor → Kel → Ash → Tor → Karn →
Ghal → Bren → Skol → Zhar → Vael → Drov → Thurn → Ulth**

The book holds 24 words — **Warbrand**, **Ghoststep**, **Kindling**, **Scholar**,
**Oathward**, **King's Purse**, **Clarity**, **Ruin**, **Anima**, **Mournfield**,
**Sunscar**, **Undertow**, **Gravemeal**, **Stormlaw**, **Hearthlight**,
**Bulwark**, **Winterking**, **The Drought**, **Nightparish**, **Stonewill**,
**Sanctum**, **Kingslayer**, **Thornparade**, and the 4-socket grail
**Worldsedge**. The law: a word only takes in plain steel, rune-order exact,
every socket filled. **Jewels** — cut stones of one to three facets — seat into
any socket for raw power, but a seated stone bars the word forever.

## The three worlds

The world is walked three times: **Normal**, **Nightmare** (−40 resistances,
the deep alphabet begins), **Hell** (−100, the world at its cruelest, mythics
abound). Each is earned by unmaking Uldrath in the one before. Level tops out
at 99; past level 30 the trees open their fifth tier — **Transcendence**.

## The legendarium (naming style)

Uniques and better are named in-world: named after people of this canon
(*Hargol's Cleaver*, *Vessira's Visage*, *Aldegar's Wedding Band*, *Quindral's
Wisdom*), places (*Kragmar's Face*, *Halvard's Rest*), or evocative original
compounds (*The Orphan Star*, *The Ferryman's Fee*, *The Silent Suitor*,
*Moonwane*, *Hoarfang*, *The Blunt Truth*). Sets: **Vargan's Warplate**,
**The Pallbearer's Garb**, **The Arcane Conclave**, **Hunter's Creed**,
**The Colossus**.

## What is deliberately generic (and fine)

Waypoints, shrines, charms (Small/Large/Grand), affix names (Sharp, Sturdy,
of Haste…), base item types (Kite Shield, Morning Star, War Pike, Bone Helm,
Sacred Armor, Titan Blade), the six classes (barbarian, rogue, sorcerer,
paladin, necromancer, druid), and dictionary-word skills (Whirlwind, Smite,
Zeal, Teleport, Meteor, Teeth, Werewolf…).

## Save-file compatibility

Profiles written before the Great Renaming are migrated on load
(`server/src/persistence.ts`): item names, set ids, rune ids inside sockets,
runeword names, quest ids, and cause-of-death labels all translate to the new
canon. The migration maps are the only place the old names may appear in code.

## The telling of it — storytellers, town talk & dungeon encounters

The world's grief is told, not just fought. Every town NPC can be **talked to**
(a D1/D2 dialogue window: a name-plate, paged text, a short list of topics),
with a **Shop / Gamble / Hire** button beneath the Talk button where it applies.
The functional folk — Torvald, Lysa, Fenwick, and the four hiring captains
(Maren, Orsavio, Hrothgar, Iseult) — each carry a slice of the world through
their topics, darkening act by act.

**The storytellers.** One elder sits by the campfire in every land — a new face
each act, talk-only, the vehicle of the main arc:

| Act | Storyteller | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| I | **Edric the Elder** | last old man of Hollowmere; the cathedral's fall, the Hollow Kings |
| II | **Halqa, the Sand-Blind** | blind oracle of Zerath; the green country drunk dry from below |
| III | **Vaol the Ferryman** | poles the drowned dead at Mirefall; Ssarim and the river's one question |
| IV | **Brother Cael, the Last Watch** | the final garrison before the Throne; Aldric, Malgrim, Uldrath, the end |

**The throughline.** Three brother-kings — Malgrim, Aldric, Uldrath — traded
their names to the dark below and now **unmake the world from underneath**,
slowly, land by land, each act the rot one stage further gone: the Mournfield
*grieves*, the Sunscar Wastes *thirst*, the Drowned Green *drowns*, and it all
narrows to the stair beneath the Hollow Throne. Cutting the rot at its root
(felling an act's ruler) only opens the road deeper — toward **Uldrath the
Unmaker** at the bottom of Act IV, and past him the bottomless Abyss. The horror
the elders name is not conquest but **consent**: the rot doesn't take a land, it
convinces it to stop being green.

**Dungeon encounters.** Most floors hide one small story out in the dark, rolled
between three kinds: a **vignette** (a fallen Warden's warning, a monk's carved
confession, boats hung in a flooded canopy — paged text and a scrap of insight,
like a tome), an **ambush** (a "survivor" who is no longer a survivor — the
dialogue turns, and the thing wearing him spills out), or a **bargain** — a pale
figure or drowned suitor offering a short boon, or a curse wearing a boon's face
("Warmth, for a memory"; "the river only ever asks once"). Encounters extend
this canon; keep them grim, original, and quiet.

## Each land its own faces — act-scoped townsfolk

Towns are no longer interchangeable. **The recruiter keeps to one act**: the
**muster post** in every town holds only that act's captain — **Maren**'s rogues
in Act I, **Orsavio**'s acolytes in II, **Hrothgar**'s warband in III,
**Iseult**'s knights in IV (the Abyss borrows the last). You hire that act's
school; any recruiter will *revive* whatever companion you already march with.

Each act also keeps **its own talk-only townsfolk** — the elder plus two locals,
met only while you walk that land:

| Act | Elder | …and two locals |
|---|---|---|
| I | Edric the Elder | **Senna the Herb-Witch** (rowan on every lintel), **Pip, the Gravedigger's Boy** (digs them twice now) |
| II | Halqa, the Sand-Blind | **Jenda, the Water-Warden** (one cup, one soul, one sundown), **Brother Ashk, the Cinder-Priest** (the good news is there is none) |
| III | Vaol the Ferryman | **Marsh-Mother Oona** (leeches the fever and the truth), **Deel, the Stilt-Walker** (won't come down off the poles) |
| IV | Brother Cael, the Last Watch | **Sister Adelheid, the Bell-Keeper** (oils a bell she prays never to ring), **Tomas, Aldric's Squire** (keeps the bright plate ready) |

They deepen the act's grief from a second angle — the domestic, the working,
the left-behind — while the recruiter and the elder carry the war and the arc.

## The rooms with doors — taverns, huts & the folk who keep them

Each act's town is dressed as its own place — Hollowmere's rain-blacked timber,
Zerath's sun-bleached adobe, Mirefall's moss-slick stilts, the Last Vigil's
ash-choked stone — and two of its buildings, **the tavern and one hut**, are
rooms you can actually walk into. Step through the door and the roof lifts away.

Someone is always **at the bar**, and always **at the hearth** — a different pair
in every land, talk-only, their moods darkening as the world does:

| Act | Barkeep (the tavern) | Resident (the hut) |
|---|---|---|
| I — Hollowmere | **Brannoch, of the Crooked Lantern** (keeps a light hung wrong on purpose, so folk stop and come in out of the dark) | **Old Mabel** (keeps her lost boy's chair and the fire high enough he'd see it from the road) |
| II — Zerath Oasis | **Sedira, the Oasis Pourer** (water's dearer than wine, so she pours wine; folk come in for the ceiling) | **Amara, the Well-Widow** (her husband calls up through the sand she filled the well with, in his own dear voice) |
| III — Mirefall Landing | **Gullimon, Keeper of the Stilt-House** (moves the good barrels to the loft as the water climbs; hears the tide-breath that isn't the tide) | **Wickem, the Reed-Cutter** (cuts the tall reeds over the drowned graves; one bled, and he's stopped going out past the third channel) |
| IV — The Last Vigil | **Old Hesk, the Last Pour** (last lit hearth this side of the Throne; strains the ash from the ale and never stops) | **Greta, Who Would Not Leave** (sweeps the ash off her step every morning; the answer to the kneeling was always no) |

The tavern's namesake **crooked lantern** swings over the room; patrons nurse
drinks at the counter; a hearth burns in the corner. The barkeep and the
resident carry the same theme the elders do — grief, thirst, drowning, the
kneeling — but from the smallest, warmest, most stubborn angle: the one lit room
that refuses to go dark.
